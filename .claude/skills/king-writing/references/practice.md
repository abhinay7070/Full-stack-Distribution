# The Writing Life: King's practice rules

Use this when the user asks how to *become* a better writer, not how to fix one text.

## The great commandment
**Read a lot, write a lot.** No shortcut.
- If you don't have time to read, you don't have the time (or the tools) to write.
- **Bad books teach what not to do.** King's example is a pulp novel that overused "zestful", which vaccinated him for life. "I can do better than this" is a milestone.
- **Great books teach** style, graceful narration, plot development, believable characters, and truth-telling.
  You can't sweep a reader away until a book has done it to you.
- **Imitating styles is a normal stage of development,** and blending them makes your own. Imitating a genre formula for money doesn't work.
- **Read in small sips and long swallows:** waiting rooms, lines, audiobooks, the treadmill. Cut TV.
- King reads 70-80 books a year and recommends 4-6 hours a day of reading plus writing.

## Output discipline

| Rule | King's number |
|---|---|
| Daily goal (beginner) | 1,000 words a day, one day off a week at most |
| Daily goal (King) | 2,000 words (about 10 pages), every day of a project, holidays included |
| First draft of a novel | No longer than ~3 months, a season |
| Rest between drafts | At least 6 weeks |
| Drafts | 2 drafts + a polish |
| Revision cut | ≥ 10% |

- **Write every day of a project.** Breaks let characters go stale and the story lose its edge.
- **Writing is at its best as inspired play.** When it starts to feel like work, the writing suffers.
- **Talent makes the idea of practice meaningless.** If there's no joy in it, find another field.

## The room and the door
- **A humble room with one requirement: a door you're willing to close.** King wrote his first two novels in a trailer laundry room with a child's desk on his thighs.
- **No phone, no TV, no games.** Shades down unless the window faces a wall. Loud music works as another way of shutting the door.
- **Same time every day,** and you leave when the quota is done. Treat it like sleep: a ritual that trains the mind to dream on cue.
- **The muse is a guy in the basement with a cigar.** You do the grunt work. Your job is to make sure he knows where you'll be every day from nine to noon, and he'll start showing up.

## Mindset
- **Writing is telepathy.** Take it seriously or go wash the car.
- **Fear is the root of bad writing.**
- **Writing classes:** they take your desire to write seriously, but vague critiques are wind, and daily critique keeps the door permanently open.
  You learn most with the door closed.
- **Interruptions don't hurt much.** The grit makes the pearl. Pressure to produce ("I hafta" instead of "I wanna") hurts more.
- **Write for joy, not money.** If you can do it for joy, you can do it forever.
- **The scariest moment is always just before you start.**

## Submitting work (adapted from the composite young writer "Frank" in the book)
- **Read the market before you submit.** Otherwise you're playing darts in a dark room.
- **Look professional:** a short cover letter (credits, a line on what the piece is, thanks for reading), clean formatting, an honest word count.
  You can't make them like your work, but you can make it easy for them to try.
- **Query letters:** plain and specific. List credits with real numbers, add one line of personality, describe the work in one line, and make a clear offer.
  Frank's casual-sounding letter took four drafts.
- **Keep a log** of every submission and every personal response, and keep stories in circulation.
- **Avoid agents who charge reading fees.** Ask any agent for their sales record.
