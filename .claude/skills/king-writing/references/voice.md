# The King Voice: measured fingerprint and devices

Use this when the user wants text that *sounds* like King (conversational, funny, blunt, warm), not only text that follows his rules.
The examples below are original, written in the style. They are not quotes.

## 1. Measured fingerprint
Measured from the book's craft chapters (~41,800 words) and memoir chapters (~25,600 words):

| Metric | Craft voice (teaching) | Memoir voice (story) | Target for your text |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean sentence length | 21.3 words (median 19) | 18.8 (median 16) | 14-22 for prose, 8-16 for email/post |
| Very short sentences (1-5 words) | 10% | 12% | ≥ 10% (the punch lines) |
| Long sentences (31+ words) | 21% | 17% | 10-20%; rhythm comes from the mix |
| One-syllable words | 63% | 65% | ≥ 60% |
| 3+ syllable words | 11% | 9% | ≤ 12% |
| -ly adverbs / 1000 words | 12.1 | 9.4 | ≤ 10 (beat the master) |
| Passive constructions / 1000 | 3.4 | 4.5 | ≤ 4 |
| Contractions / 1000 | 27.3 | 19.5 | ≥ 15 |
| Em-dashes / 1000 | 7.6 | 6.8 | 3-8 |
| Parentheses / 1000 | 6.8 | 4.3 | 2-7 (asides) |
| "you/your" / 1000 | 22.7 | 5.4 | high when teaching, low when narrating |
| "I/me/my" / 1000 | 30.0 | 51.0 | first person, unashamed |
| Questions / 1000 | 3.8 | 1.9 | rhetorical questions to the reader |

**Reading:** short words in long, loose sentences, then a short sentence that lands.
He talks to *you*. He interrupts himself in parentheses. The register is close to spoken American, with some profanity.

## 2. Device catalog

| # | Device | What it does | Original example in the style |
|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | **The punch sentence after the long one** | Sets the rhythm. A long, loose build, then one blunt line on its own. | "I tried the CRM, the spreadsheet, the sticky notes on the monitor, and a system my cousin swore by that involved colored index cards. None of it stuck. The notebook did." |
| V2 | **The parenthetical aside** | Makes it sound like talk, adds a wink, admits a flaw | "I answered every email that day (well, most of them)." |
| V3 | **The em-dash swerve** | Changes direction mid-thought | "The plan was simple—it always is, the night before." |
| V4 | **Concrete brand-name specifics** | Makes it real. He names the actual objects people own: the typewriter model, the hotel, the song on the radio. | "a dented Thermos of gas-station coffee," not "a beverage" |
| V5 | **The homely simile** | A fresh comparison drawn from ordinary life | "as useful as a screen door on a submarine" is a cliché; build new ones: "as welcome as a fire alarm at a funeral" |
| V6 | **The rule-of-three list with a comic last item** | Deflation | "Writers need a desk, a door, and a spouse who doesn't ask how it's going." |
| V7 | **Direct address and a dare** | Turns the reader into a partner | "You know this already. You just haven't done it." |
| V8 | **Self-deprecation as credibility** | He confesses his own sins ("I'm just another ordinary sinner") | "I've written that sentence myself. More than once." |
| V9 | **Coined labels for recurring ideas** | Constant Reader, the All-Story Draft, Ideal Reader, the muse as a cigar-chomping basement guy | Name your own concepts and reuse the names |
| V10 | **The callback closer** | A short last line that echoes an earlier image | He opens with the choice between writing and washing the car, and ends the chapter: "Wash the car, maybe." |
| V11 | **Extended metaphor carried through** | The toolbox, the fossil, the door. One image does the work for a whole section. | Pick one image and carry it to the end |
| V12 | **Anecdote before principle** | Story first (the uncle and the screwdriver), lesson second | Open with a scene, then say what it means |
| V13 | **Interjections and exclamations used sparingly** | "Fuhgeddaboudit!", "Please oh please." A spike of energy. | No more than one per page |
| V14 | **The blunt verdict** | "That's not prose, that's an instruction manual." | Judge plainly. No "arguably". |
| V15 | **A hard number inside soft prose** | 2,000 words a day, 10%, 6 weeks, 39 of 50 one-syllable words | One exact figure per section anchors trust |
| V16 | **One honest admission at the emotional peak** | Ends a heavy section with plain feeling, not a moral | "It was the best I could do." |

## 3. Register rules
- **Plain American speech.** Write "folks", "stuff", "pretty good", "a hell of a", with occasional profanity. Match the profanity to the audience; the plainness itself is not optional.
- **No academic hedging.** King says "I believe" and "I think" as a stance, then commits. He never writes "it could be argued".
- **Warm toward the reader, merciless toward bad prose.** Encouraging, but no lies: "there are lots of bad writers".
- **Humor through specificity and deflation**, not jokes for their own sake.
- **Mix teaching and storytelling.** The principle arrives inside an anecdote.

## 4. Anti-patterns (these break the voice)
- Corporate abstractions (leverage, synergy, solutions), and words like "delve" or "tapestry".
- Every sentence the same length. A wall of 20-word sentences reads like a machine wrote it.
- Stacked adjectives before a noun.
- Summaries that repeat the point ("In conclusion..."). King ends on an image or a short line.
- Emoji, bullet lists in narrative prose, and too many exclamation marks.
- Telling the reader what to feel ("This is a heartwarming story").

## 5. Voice self-check (run before shipping)
- [ ] Is there at least one sentence of 5 words or fewer per ~100 words?
- [ ] Is there a concrete, named object instead of an abstraction?
- [ ] Is there at least one aside (parenthesis or dash) that sounds like speech?
- [ ] Is every -ly adverb either cut or defensible?
- [ ] Does it end on an image or a short line, not a summary?
- [ ] Read aloud: does it sound like a person talking across a kitchen table?
