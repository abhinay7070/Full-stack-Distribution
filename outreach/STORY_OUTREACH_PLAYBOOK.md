# Story Outreach Playbook: designed to land one job

Built from three independent drafts (Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT), the king-writing skill and the pipeline data.
Sample built with this playbook: `outreach/sample_newton_strowger.md`. Stories: `stories/STORY_BANK.md`.

## 1. What we learned from three AI systems

| Source | Best idea (keep) | Fatal flaw (never repeat) |
|---|---|---|
| Gemini | **Match the story's mechanism to the company's product** (Strowger → phone AI) | Invented facts, motives and numbers; talked down to the founder |
| ChatGPT | **A source for every claim; honest intent; use real credentials** | Gated on a reply, so most stories never get told; flat hooks |
| Claude | **Craft, verification, a daily cadence, a specific offer** | Generic analogies; no proof of work |
| **Nobody** | — | **Did real work for the company before asking.** That is the gap this playbook fills. |

## 2. Five rules
1. **True, or told as legend.** Every claim has a source. Where history is contested, say "he believed" or "the story goes". A founder who googles one fake detail never replies.
2. **The mechanism matches.** The story's core move must be what the company's product does (remove the gatekeeper, test before it breaks, create a new habit). A story that is merely interesting is decoration.
3. **Honest intent from Email 1.** P.S. "I'm applying for your X role. This is the cover letter, in three short parts." This removes the "who is this stranger?" suspicion and turns the curiosity into a frame.
4. **Proof of work, never claims of work.** Email 3 carries real, named results built for *them* (5 accounts, links). It must never contain an invented number.
5. **Respect.** Never tell a founder their hiring plan is ironic or wrong. Show a better way and let them conclude it.

## 3. Sequence shape: 3 + 1 (not 4 in a row)

| # | Day | Job | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | D1 | Story setup ending on the cliffhanger. P.S. states intent. | Hook plus honesty. Works even if it's the only email read. |
| 2 | D2 | Resolution, then **the bridge: their product is the hero's invention** | The mechanism match lands here |
| 3 | D3 | Their problem, then **proof of work (5 real accounts)**, then the offer and a small ask | The reason to reply |
| 4 | D10 | No story. 5 more accounts, yours either way. Ask what was wrong. | A give-first follow-up that brings the thread back. Never "just bumping". |

Why not 4 days of story: four daily emails before any value is a lot to ask of a stranger. Three daily emails plus one useful follow-up keeps the rhythm and ends on a gift, not an ask.

## 4. Per-company workflow (R1, about 40 min each)
1. **Read the pipeline row:** `one_liner`, open role, raise, headcount, outbound stack (`sublists/master_priority.csv`).
2. **Map to a mechanism theme**, then pick the story (`STORY_BANK.md` theme table).
3. **Write the bridge line:** "{Company} is {hero}'s {invention}."
4. **Proof of work (20-30 min):** find 10 real accounts *their* buyers would be. Examples: dental groups hiring front-desk staff for Newton; teams that just shipped a voice agent for Bluejay. Use job boards, funding events and the pipeline itself. 5 go in Email 3, 5 in Email 4.
5. **Gates:** verified email (Clay) · `king_lint` ≥ 90 with no placeholders · every story claim sourced · one sequence per company · "I applied" only if you applied.
6. **Log** in Outreach_Log: story id, theme, send days, reply.

## 5. Scale: tiers

| Ring (pipeline) | Count | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| R1 Sniper (exact role, A-tier) | ~100-150 picked from 744 | Full playbook: matched story + proof of work |
| R2 Rifle | Next ~300 | Story from the theme bucket + a bridge line; no proof list (offer it instead) |
| R3 | Rest | One short signal email, no story |

## 7. Measurement and kill rules
- Track replies by story, theme and sequence shape. After **30 sends per story**, drop any story under 3% replies and double down on the best.
- Test the one real disagreement: daily cadence (ours) vs. reply-gated (ChatGPT) on matched groups of ~20 each.
- Re-read every reply, including the no's. "Which part was wrong" answers are free product research.
