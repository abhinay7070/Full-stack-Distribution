The gift was a little green pickle charm.

People climbed the stairs. Then more people climbed, because they saw others doing it. By the end of the fair Heinz had given away something like a million, and the exhibitors on his floor, who had been sitting in an empty gallery until then, thanked him for it.

He didn't move the booth or change the product. He went where people already were and gave them one small reason to climb.

In the summer of 1893, a young monk arrived in Chicago from India to speak at the Parliament of the World's Religions.

Then he found out he couldn't. Delegates needed credentials from a recognized organization. He had none, and very little money.

A Harvard professor named John Henry Wright met him and wrote to the organizers himself. The line he's remembered for: asking this man for credentials was like asking the sun for its right to shine.

On 11 September the monk stood up and began with five words. "Sisters and brothers of America." The hall rose.

His name was Swami Vivekananda. Nobody in that hall had heard of him. They remembered what he said.
