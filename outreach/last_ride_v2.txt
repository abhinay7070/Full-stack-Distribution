On 16 January 1913, a clerk in the accounts office of the Madras Port Trust posted a letter to a stranger at Cambridge. It went by ship.

He had no degree. He earned £20 a year. The letter began with a few plain lines about himself. Then it was pages of formulas.

G. H. Hardy nearly set it aside. That evening he went through it again with Littlewood. He later wrote that some of the formulas had to be true, because if they weren't, no one would have had the imagination to invent them.

The clerk was Srinivasa Ramanujan. A year later he was at Cambridge.

Slow, from far away, from nobody. It worked because it reached the one man who could read it.
