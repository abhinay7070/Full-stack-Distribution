# The Switch (Strowger): generic 4-email sequence for scale

Tokens: {Name}, {Company}, {role}. Hook A subject: `an undertaker, a switchboard, and {company}` (add "It ends with {Company}." before the sign-off in Email 1). Hook B subject: `kansas city, 1889`.
Sources: Wikipedia (Almon Brown Strowger): newspaper trigger, operator story told as his belief, collar box and pins (Kansas City Star, 1928), patent 1891, first commercial exchange La Porte IN 3 Nov 1892 (~75 subscribers).

## Email 1

```
{Name},

Around 1889, a Kansas City undertaker named Almon Strowger opened the newspaper and read that a friend had died.

Another undertaker was handling the funeral.

It wasn't the first time business had gone around him. Strowger thought he knew why, and it wasn't his prices or his service.

It was a woman at a switchboard.

I'll tell you tomorrow.

Abhinay
```

## Email 2

```
{Name},

In 1889 you couldn't dial anyone yourself. You picked up the phone and asked an operator to put you through.

Strowger believed the local operator was a rival undertaker's wife. Ask her for "the undertaker" and you got her husband.

He didn't complain to the phone company. He decided to get rid of the operator.

The story goes that his first model was a round collar box and a handful of straight pins.

Tomorrow: what the pins became.

Abhinay
```

## Email 3

```
{Name},

In 1891 Strowger patented an automatic telephone exchange. The caller chose the number. A machine made the connection. Nobody in the middle could send the call somewhere else.

On November 3, 1892, the first exchange built on his design opened in La Porte, Indiana. About 75 people signed up.

He never out-argued the operator. He made her unnecessary.

Most early startups have an operator of their own. It isn't a person. It's whatever sits between them and the buyer: the front desk, the inbox that filters cold email, the form nobody answers. Their sales team spends its days asking someone to put them through.

Tomorrow I'll tell you why I sent you this.

Abhinay
```

```

Week 1: who to reach, by name.
Week 2: the first sequence, live.
Week 3: meetings on your calendar.

Worth 15 minutes this week? Or tell me which part I got wrong.
```
